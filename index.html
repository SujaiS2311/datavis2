01 / THE STORY

Small creatures.
Extraordinary ecosystems.

Insects are vital to pollination, food webs, nutrient recycling and freshwater ecosystems. Yet the places we observe them tell only part of the story: biodiversity recording depends on the effort of observers as well as the insects themselves.

This website explores three orders in Atlas of Living Australia (ALA) occurrence exports from 2023–2025. We compare recorded locations, distinct species-level names and observation timing—not absolute animal populations.

Why insects matter

01

Pollination

Many bees, butterflies and moths move pollen between flowers, helping plants reproduce.

02

Food webs

Insects feed birds, reptiles and other animals, linking plants, soil and higher consumers.

03

Freshwater health

Dragonflies and damselflies spend parts of their lives in freshwater habitats and are tied to wetland ecosystems.

05 / TRANSPARENCY

How to read this evidence

Sources

ALA occurrence downloads: Lepidoptera, Hymenoptera, and Odonata. Also uses the provided ALA species checklist and ALA monthly summary, and a separate threatened-species CSV whose publisher should be confirmed before final submission.

Map outlines: rowanhogan/australian-states GeoJSON; check attribution/licensing before final submission.

Methods and limitations

Occurrence observations were limited to 2023–2025, valid months and approximate Australian latitude/longitude bounds. Species richness counts distinct nonblank scientific names with taxonRank = species; these are not a reconciled taxonomic checklist. The dot map is a seeded subsample of up to 700 records per group per year. State circles indicate administrative totals rather than exact locations.

Data warning: Public reporting effort, identification quality and survey methods are uneven. The separate 2023–2025 monthly CSV contains beetles and may not match the filtered ZIP totals. This draft is intended for tutor critique and should be refined after feedback.

Chart specifications are human-readable JSON in the charts/ folder. This web page uses Vega-Lite and Vega-Embed. Project notes.
